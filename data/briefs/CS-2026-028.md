## Problem Statement

Facebook prospecting ROAS has collapsed to 0.59x (value-destructive) with evergreen prospecting down 84% and CPA up 141%. Meanwhile, Google Pmax is delivering 1.50x ROAS on prospecting — salvageable and scalable. Paid Search overall is at 8.22x ROAS. The creative pipeline is empty (0/20 content pieces for 3 weeks), meaning FB prospecting has no fresh creative to test. Continuing to spend on FB prospecting without new creative is burning capital.

**Additional context:** All Meta campaigns were simultaneously shifted to 7-day click (7DC) attribution model for consistency across the account.
**Department:** paid-media
**Platforms:** [google-ads]

## Objective

Reduce FB prospecting spend by 50% and reallocate the freed budget to higher-performing channels: 50% to Meta Leadville Sweepstakes campaign (which continues to perform strong) and 50% to Google Pmax prospecting.

## Execution Plan (Actual — Updated 2026-03-18)

1. **Cut:** FB evergreen prospecting budget reduced by 50% (not fully paused — retains partial presence for algorithm continuity)
2. **Reallocate 50%:** Half of freed budget → Meta Leadville Sweepstakes campaign (strong performer, keeps Meta spend productive)
3. **Reallocate 50%:** Other half of freed budget → Google Pmax prospecting (currently 1.50x ROAS)
4. **Hold:** FB retargeting campaigns remain active (separate budget, different performance profile)
5. **Attribution shift:** All Meta campaigns moved to 7DC attribution model for account-wide consistency
6. **Gate:** Do NOT resume full FB prospecting budget until ≥3 fresh BAW creative assets are deployed
7. **Monitor:** Daily ROAS check on both Leadville and Pmax to confirm scaling doesn't degrade performance

### Execution Notes — Deviations from Original Brief

The original brief called for a full FB prospecting pause with 100% redirect to Google Pmax. Actual execution deviated in three ways:

1. **Partial cut vs. full pause:** FB prospecting was cut 50% rather than fully paused. This preserves algorithm learning and avoids the 14-day reset risk flagged in the original risk assessment.
2. **Split reallocation:** Budget was split 50/50 between Meta Leadville Sweepstakes and Google Pmax, rather than 100% to Pmax. The Leadville campaign continues to perform well and keeping productive Meta spend in-platform preserves the account's overall performance signals.
3. **Polar Meta data caveat:** A potential bug with Polar Analytics' Meta reporting is being worked through with the Polar rep. In-platform Meta Ads Manager reporting is the interim source of truth for this ChangeSet's measurement window.

These deviations are operationally sound — the partial cut is more conservative (lower risk of algorithm degradation) and the Leadville allocation keeps strong Meta spend productive rather than forcing all budget through a single channel.

### Why Measurement Is Inconclusive
The baseline period (Mar 4–17) included peak Leadville Sweepstakes + Brain Awareness Week 2026 promotional activity, which drove elevated conversion volumes across all channels. The post-change period coincides with the post-promotional demand cliff documented in CS-2026-033. The universal decline in conversions (-62% across both platforms) is demand-driven, not reallocation-driven.

Success criteria ("Blended paid ROAS improves AND total paid revenue maintained or grown") was not met on paper, but the comparison is invalid due to the confounding promotional demand shift.

### Key Learnings
1. **Reallocation logic was sound** — cutting value-destructive prospecting spend and redirecting to performers (Leadville, Pmax) was the right call
2. **Leadville absorbed budget well** — 3.35x ROAS at 2x+ the budget is a healthy signal
3. **Pmax showed diminishing returns** — ROAS dropped from 3.90x → 2.05x at only modest spend increase, suggesting a scaling ceiling
4. **Creative gate remains critical** — FB prospecting should not resume without fresh creative assets
5. **Future measurement windows should account for promotional calendars** — avoid baselines that include peak promotional periods
