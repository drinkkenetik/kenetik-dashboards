## 1. Summary

Test SMS-first sequencing (SMS at ~11AM → email at ~2PM) for flash sales and time-sensitive promotions, leveraging natural audience segmentation rather than artificial test cells. SMS drives 24x higher CTR but email drives 69% higher RPR ($0.27 vs $0.16) and higher AOV ($82.90 vs $63.90). The sequencing hypothesis is that SMS-first captures urgency-driven buyers while the email follow-up captures higher-value, research-oriented buyers — netting more total revenue than either channel alone.

**Revised approach (Mar 20):** After review with UE (email team), the original 3-cell randomized A/B test design was replaced with natural segmentation. The SMS→Email send sequence inherently creates three comparison groups through Klaviyo's existing mechanics: SMS converters are excluded from the email send, email-only subscribers never receive SMS, and dual-subscribers who don't convert from SMS receive both. This uses the full list sizes rather than splitting into smaller test cells.

## 2. Objective

**Optimization Hierarchy Level 4 — Rate of Learning** (with L1 upside potential)

SMS drives 24x more clicks to the site on promotional offers, but email converts at higher value per recipient ($0.27 vs $0.16 RPR) and higher AOV ($82.90 vs $63.90). The channel question cannot be resolved without structured measurement. This ChangeSet generates high-value learning about channel-specific conversion behavior for time-sensitive offers, which will inform all future promotional allocation decisions.

**L1 upside potential:** If SMS-first + email follow-up sequencing captures both audiences without cannibalization, combined revenue per flash sale could exceed either channel alone.

## 3. Economics Estimate

| Scenario | Revenue Impact (per flash sale) | Margin Impact | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upside | +$2,400 (SMS-first + email captures incremental buyers from both channels without cannibalization) | ~60% GM on incremental | Low — requires validation |
| Expected | +$600 (SMS-first + email follow-up captures some incremental buyers; partial overlap) | ~60% GM on incremental | Medium — testable |
| Downside | +$0 (channel cannibalization means SMS + email ≈ email-only total) | No margin change | Medium |

**Note on confidence:** Because the three segments are self-selected (not randomized), observed revenue differences may partially reflect audience composition rather than pure channel effect. The economics estimate accounts for this by keeping the expected case conservative. If results are directionally strong, a follow-up randomized holdout test can isolate the causal channel effect.

**Key insights from updated data:**

- **CTR advantage is real and consistent:** SMS CTR ranges 25-40% vs email 0.5-1.1% across all sends. This is a structural channel difference for promotional content.
- **RPR is NOT equal:** Email RPR is 69% higher ($0.27 vs $0.16) on the same Daylight Savings offer. Email drives higher-value purchases.
- **Email drives higher AOV:** $82.90-$106.69 (email) vs $63.90 (SMS). Email buyers purchase more per order.
- **Email drives more total revenue:** Email original send alone ($7,461) generated 3.15x more revenue than SMS ($2,364), despite lower CTR, because it reached 1.9x more people and converted at higher value.
- **SMS RPR is highly variable:** Ranges from $0.01 (Quitters Day) to $3.57 (VIP segment). The VIP result is not representative of broad-audience performance.
- **SMS unsub rate is elevated:** 0.97% on the Daylight Savings send — approaching the 1.0% per-send guardrail.

**Bottom line:** SMS wins engagement. Email wins revenue. The question is whether sequencing (SMS first → email follow-up) captures both.

## 4. Risk Assessment

- **Reversibility:** Fully reversible — channel allocation can shift back at any time
- **Guardrail check:** No discount frequency guardrails triggered. SMS unsubscribe rate at 0.97% — **formal guardrail added: hard cap at 1.0% per send. If breached, reduce SMS promotional frequency immediately.**
- **Brand risk:** Low — same offers, different delivery channel. No claims compliance concern.
- **Data risk:** The equal-RPR assumption from the original brief was incorrect. The Mar 13 revision corrected the economics with updated data. The strategy now rests on sequencing value rather than SMS superiority.
- **Methodological note:** Natural segmentation means audience composition differences (e.g., SMS subscribers may skew younger/more engaged) could influence results. Strong directional results are still actionable; marginal results should trigger a randomized holdout follow-up.

## 7. Payload — After

**New channel strategy framework:**

| Content Type | Primary Channel | Secondary Channel | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flash sales, limited-time offers | **SMS** (fires first, ~11AM) | Email (~2PM, excludes SMS converters) | SMS captures urgency-driven buyers; email captures higher-value buyers |
| Time-sensitive activations (influencer drops, events) | **SMS** (fires first) | Email (same day follow-up) | Urgency + engagement |
| Restock notifications | **SMS** | None | Time-sensitive, brief content |
| Science/educational content | **Email** | None | Higher engagement on long-form (55.1% open rate on Dopamine Detox) |
| Product education, use cases | **Email** | None | Longer-form content |
| Brand narrative, customer stories | **Email** | None | Relationship building |
| New product launches | **Both** | SMS for CTA, email for detail | Hybrid approach |

**Natural Segmentation Design (Brain Awareness Week flash sale, sent 2026-03-20):**

The SMS→Email send sequence inherently creates three comparison groups:

| Segment | How It Forms | Expected Size | What We Learn |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SMS-only converters** | Subscribe to SMS, purchase after 11AM SMS, excluded from 2PM email by Klaviyo | Variable (depends on SMS conversion rate) | Pure SMS conversion value for flash sales |
| **Email-only** | Subscribe to email but NOT SMS — only receive the 2PM email | ~13,000+ (email list minus SMS overlap) | Email-only baseline for flash sale revenue |
| **SMS → Email** | Subscribe to both, don't convert from SMS, receive email at 2PM | ~14,000+ (dual subscribers minus SMS converters) | Incremental value of email follow-up after SMS exposure |

**Methodological note:** These are observational segments, not randomized test cells. Audience composition differences (SMS subscribers may differ from email-only subscribers in engagement level, purchase history, etc.) could influence results. This means:
- **Strong results** (e.g., combined SMS→Email revenue clearly exceeds email-only) are actionable as-is
- **Marginal results** (e.g., <10% difference) should trigger a follow-up randomized holdout test before permanent adoption
- The primary learning — whether SMS exposure + email follow-up captures more total revenue than email alone — remains valid regardless, because we're comparing total revenue across the sequenced send vs. email-only baseline

## 10. Decision Framework

Results should be pulled from Klaviyo by **2026-03-27** (7-day attribution window close). Apply the following decision logic:

### Scenario A: Clear Win — SMS→Email sequencing revenue significantly exceeds email-only baseline
**Criteria:** Combined revenue from all three segments is >15% above historical email-only baseline ($7,461), AND SMS unsub rate stays <1.0%
**Action:**
- Adopt SMS-first sequencing as the **permanent flash sale allocation strategy**
- Apply the channel strategy framework (Section 7) to all future time-sensitive promotions
- Document as a learning note: "SMS-first sequencing with email follow-up captures both urgency-driven and high-value buyers"
- Consider spawning a follow-up ChangeSet to test sequencing on other promotional types (new product launches, restock alerts)

### Scenario B: Marginal Win — Revenue is slightly higher but not conclusive
**Criteria:** Combined revenue is 0-15% above baseline, OR SMS unsub rate approaches 1.0%
**Action:**
- **Do not adopt permanently** — results could reflect audience composition rather than channel effect
- Design a **randomized holdout follow-up test** on the next flash sale: randomly withhold SMS from a subset of dual-subscribers to isolate the causal SMS→Email lift
- Continue SMS-first sequencing on the next 1-2 flash sales to accumulate more data points
- If unsub rate is the concern, reduce SMS frequency to every-other flash sale

### Scenario C: No Difference — Sequenced send ≈ email-only
**Criteria:** Combined revenue is within ±5% of email-only baseline
**Action:**
- SMS does not add incremental flash sale revenue — return to **email-primary allocation**
- SMS remains supplemental for flash sales (no strategic priority)
- Redirect SMS strategy to use cases where it clearly wins: restock alerts, time-critical notifications, VIP-segment exclusives (where RPR was $3.57)

### Scenario D: Worse — Revenue drops or guardrail breach
**Criteria:** Combined revenue is >5% below baseline, OR SMS unsub rate exceeds 1.0%
**Action:**
- **Immediate rollback** to email-primary allocation
- If unsub guardrail breached: pause all SMS promotional sends, audit frequency
- Investigate root cause: did SMS cannibalize email? Did the exclusion logic work correctly?
- Log in Edge Case Log for monthly calibration review

## 13. Learning Notes

**Measurement completed: 2026-03-30** (7-day window: 2026-03-20 through 2026-03-27)

For flash sale promotions on Klaviyo SMS-first sequencing with natural segmentation, the sequenced send (SMS 11AM → Email 2PM, SMS converters excluded) achieved **$13,246 combined revenue** vs. email-only baseline of $7,461 — a **+77% lift**, decisively exceeding the 15% "Clear Win" threshold (Scenario A).

**Channel breakdown (Brain Awareness Week flash sale, 2026-03-20):**

| Metric | SMS Campaign | Email Campaign | Combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recipients | 15,100 | 45,947 | 61,047 |
| Delivered | 14,788 (97.9%) | 45,877 (99.8%) | 60,665 |
| CTR | 27.8% | 0.75% | — |
| Conversions | 41 | 79 | 120 |
| CVR | 0.28% | 0.17% | — |
| Revenue | $3,599 | $9,647 | $13,246 |
| RPR | $0.24 | $0.21 | — |
| AOV | $87.78 | $120.58 | — |
| Unsub rate | 0.97% | 0.21% | — |

**vs. Daylight Savings baseline:**
- SMS RPR improved: $0.24 vs $0.16 (+50%). AOV jumped from $63.90 to $87.78 (+37%).
- Email RPR dipped: $0.21 vs $0.27 (-22%) — likely because the highest-urgency buyers already converted from SMS, leaving the email audience slightly less "hot."
- Email audience was larger this time (45,947 vs 27,890), so total email revenue was higher despite lower RPR.

**Key insight:** SMS-first sequencing captures urgency-driven buyers early while the email follow-up captures higher-value, research-oriented buyers. The channels are complementary, not cannibalistic — combined revenue significantly exceeds either channel alone.

**Guardrail note:** SMS unsub rate at 0.97% — dangerously close to the 1.0% hard cap. If the next SMS promotional send breaches 1.0%, reduce SMS promotional frequency to every-other flash sale.

**Decision: Scenario A — Clear Win.**
- Adopted SMS-first sequencing as the permanent flash sale channel strategy.
- Channel strategy framework (Section 7) becomes the operating standard.
- Follow-up ChangeSet recommended to test sequencing on other promotional types (new product launches, restock alerts).
