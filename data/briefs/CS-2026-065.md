## Problem

Two 2026 papers landed that materially change what Kenetik can say about its own product, and the regulatory backdrop is actively enforcing on adjacent ketone claims.

**1. Martyn et al. 2026 (J Appl Physiol, PMID 41962970, n=8 RCT)**
Exogenous ketone ester benefit on endurance performance is **metabolic-state-conditional**, not universal. Blanket "improves endurance" claims are no longer supported by the best available evidence. Several idea-status posts in D13 currently rely on exactly this framing.

**2. Frenser et al. 2026 (Sci Rep, PMID 41957196)**
Documents an MCT-to-glucose ketogenesis threshold — approximately **1.5g glucose per 1g MCT** — above which ketogenesis is suppressed. Kenetik's carb-forward SKUs may cross this threshold, which would undermine on-pack/landing-page ketogenic-support messaging.

**3. FTC enforcement context (D17)**
Active enforcement wave against MetaTrim and Jelly Lean for BHB performance claims. Regulatory tolerance for loose ketone claims is low right now.

Content pipeline is holding 10 idea-status social posts in D13 that should not advance until the audit is complete.

## Scope

1. **KME endurance claims audit** — all social, email, paid, website. Every universal "improves endurance" claim is flagged and rewritten to specify metabolic-state condition (fasted / glycogen-depleted / pre-race).
2. **MCT:glucose ratio review** — compute ratio for each SKU; identify any SKUs that cross the Frenser threshold. Decide: reformulate, re-message, or retire that SKU-level claim.
3. **Workflow update** — add a "science-contradiction" trigger to `claims-compliance.md` so a new peer-reviewed paper against a live claim auto-routes to this pattern.
4. **Content unblock** — approve or rewrite the 10 idea-status posts in D13 once audit is complete.
