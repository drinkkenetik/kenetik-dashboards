## Problem Statement

27-84 subscribers cancel per month citing "too much product already" (8-23.7% of all cancels,
depending on sample window -- see Data-Quality Finding below)
[src: data/voc/cancellations-corpus.json; diagnostic/retention-teardown.md]. This is churn where
the customer is not rejecting the product, only the current cadence or quantity -- the most
directly interceptable segment of the three covered in this package.

## Data-Quality Finding

The original diagnostic's 7-day/n=72 snapshot sized oversupply at 8% of cancels. A fuller,
six-week cancellation-reason log (n=401, 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-12) puts it at 23.7% (95 of 401
events, combining both "Have too much product already" and "I have too much product already"
reason-string variants) [src: data/voc/cancellations-corpus.json]. This is roughly 3x the
original estimate and, if accurate, makes this the highest-leverage of the three interventions
in this package, not the smallest. We size this CS against the higher, current-data estimate as
the primary case and flag the original 8% figure as a conservative floor. Recommend
productionizing weighted monthly churn-reason tracking (an open action item already named in
`diagnostic/retention-teardown.md`) so future sizing doesn't require a manual re-pull like this
one.

## Hypothesis

A single pre-cancellation intercept screen -- presented once, before cancellation completes --
offering skip-next-shipment, downsize/reduce-frequency, swap-product, pause-up-to-90-days, and
gift-to-a-friend will recover 20-35% of the oversupply-driven cancellation segment (7-29
subscribers/month depending on which segment-size case applies), consistent with the 15-30%
industry-benchmark range cited in the since-archived `CS-2026-024` (2026-03 pause/skip test,
not present in the current tracker.json, likely superseded during the v3 migration), which also
observed that subscribers who pause resume 3-5x more often than subscribers who cancel outright.

## Resolution Path (Diagnose / Remove / Refine / Add)

- **Diagnose:** The customer is signaling a cadence/quantity mismatch, not product rejection --
  a mismatch is fixable without losing the customer.
- **Remove:** N/A -- no existing mechanism is causing this; the gap is the absence of a
  lighter-weight option at the moment of cancellation.
- **Refine:** Refines the existing Recharge cancellation flow (adds one screen before the final
  cancel confirmation) rather than building new infrastructure; Recharge natively supports
  skip/pause/frequency-change, so this is largely a flow-sequencing and copy change, not new
  engineering.
- **Add:** The "gift a shipment to a friend" option is the one genuinely new mechanism (redirects
  a shipment as a referral) -- justified as a low-cost, high-optionality addition that converts a
  potential full loss into, at minimum, a referral lead, at the cost of one additional card on
  the intercept screen.

## Actions

1. Confirm Recharge's native skip/pause/frequency-change/product-swap capabilities are enabled
   on the current plan (verify -- do not assume all four are available by default).
2. Build the single intercept screen (not a multi-step gauntlet) presented before the final
   cancellation confirmation, with all five options as equal-weight cards plus a clear "none of
   these, still cancel" exit path (no dark patterns, no forced multi-click flow).
3. Copy (brand-voice-checked, no health/performance claims): see
   `analysis/retention-quickwin-package.md` Section 8.4 for full text.
4. Instrument: track which option (if any) each customer selects, and track 90-day resume rate
   for pause selections and repeat-cancel rate for skip/downsize selections (to catch "pause as
   delayed cancel" as a false-positive save).

## Measurement Plan

- **Primary metric:** monthly count of "too much product already" cancel reason, and overall
  voluntary cancellation rate on this reason
- **Secondary metric:** 90-day resume rate for pause selections (target >=50%, per CS-2026-024
  precedent's success-gate framing); repeat-cancel rate within 60 days for skip/downsize
  selections (catches delayed-churn-via-pause false positives)
- **Window:** 30 days for adoption/immediate save rate; 90 days for resume-rate validation
- **Success gate:** oversupply cancel-reason count down >=20% AND 90-day resume rate >=40%

## Risk Assessment

- **Risk:** Some pause/skip selections may be a stepping stone to eventual cancellation rather
  than a true save (delayed churn, not prevented churn).
- **Mitigation:** Track 90-day resume rate; if resume rate falls below ~40% at the 60-day mark,
  treat as a signal to revisit messaging or narrow the pause window.
- **Rollback:** Remove the intercept screen from the cancellation flow; Recharge's default
  cancel path is unaffected and reverts immediately.
