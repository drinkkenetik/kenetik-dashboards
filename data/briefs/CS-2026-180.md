## Closing Rationale

CS-2026-180 was generated off a 2026-06-04 daily-pulse / anomaly signal asserting that the First-Time Post-Purchase (FTPP) 2026 flow was dark for over a month, and recommended a manual bridge nurture to ~1,300 first-time buyers since 2026-05-05 (projected ~$5,700 M1 recovery on the May cohort).

**The premise is false.** The flow is live and performing:

- **Active flow:** `UE | First Time Post Purchase 2026` (`T6qbEb`), live since 2026-05-01, replacing the now-`draft` `UE | First Time Post Purchase` (`V6DbAb`)
- **Strategy in production:** Text-based founder-voice emails, segmented by purchase type
- **7-day performance:** 590 recipients / 589 delivered / RPR $1.13
- **Audience coverage:** First-time buyers since 2026-05-05 are entering the new flow as expected

No bridge send is required. No customer impact occurred. The proposed manual intervention would have duplicated messaging the cohort already received.

## Why this was caught here, not earlier

The `sync_klaviyo.py` per-flow report fetch for `T6qbEb` failed transiently and set `stats_email_14d` to null. Downstream D02/Lifecycle scan read null as zero, declared the flow dark, and the standing M1-decline performance-memory hypothesis confirmed the false signal rather than interrogating it. The same state file's `placed_order_14d.by_attributed_flow` showed `T6qbEb` attributing $788.94 over the same window — a contradiction the scan never reconciled.

Full root cause and fix in EC-2026-009 (`cgo-logs/edge-cases/EC-2026-009.md`).

Capture in Performance Memory: lifecycle flow-liveness scans must treat null per-flow stats as a fetch failure (not a zero), and must reconcile per-flow stats against attribution evidence in the same state file before escalating "flow dark" recommendations.
